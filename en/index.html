Profissional 2030 · AI Auditor program · 8 lessons

AI Auditor: find, classify, and explain the risks of the AI your company already uses

Your company already uses AI: apps built with vibe coding, agents on WhatsApp, email integrations, spreadsheets with customer data. In eight lessons, you learn to ask for authorization, build the inventory, classify each risk with evidence, and hand leadership a report with a 30-day plan.

Start with lesson 1 See the Profissional 2030 program

  • 8templates to copy
  • 12flaws planted in the lab

The lessons

In the order of real work: authorization, inventory, triage, three review fronts (apps, agents, data), governance, and the report.

  1. Lesson 1 · 18 minThe AI auditor: what you assess, what you don't, and the written authorizationThe company already uses AI in places leadership doesn't even know about. Someone needs to look with a method, show the evidence, and say what to do first. That is the AI auditor. Before you open any system, you agree in writing on what you will look at, what you won't, and who authorized it.
  2. Lesson 2 · 20 minAI inventory: everything the company uses, with an ownerNo one protects what they don't know exists. The inventory is the list of everything that uses AI in the company: apps, agents, integrations, spreadsheets that feed the AI, and the tools employees use on their own. Every row has an owner. Without an inventory, the triage in lesson 3 becomes a guess.
  3. Lesson 3 · 25 minRisk triage: one score and one piece of evidence for each itemWith the inventory done, leadership has only one question: what do I fix first? Triage answers with a simple matrix, based on the OWASP lists for AI applications and for agents. Each item gets a category, an impact score and an exposure score, a deadline, and proof of what you saw. Without proof, it doesn't go in.
  4. Lesson 4 · 25 minVibe Audit: reviewing the app the AI builtBuilding an app with AI has become easy: the manager describes it, and the tool delivers a website with a database in one afternoon. What the AI doesn't do on its own is close the doors. In this lesson you review one of these apps just by reading it: exposed secret, missing login, open database, and an old part. You diagnose; the team fixes.
  5. Lesson 5 · 25 minAgent Guard: checking agents that act on their ownAn assistant answers; an agent acts: it creates an order, gives a discount, sends an email. The more an agent can do on its own, the tighter its limits need to be. In this lesson you check five controls, the defense against hidden instructions, and where the critical rules live: in the system, not just in the prompt text.
  6. Lesson 6 · 25 minData Guard: data, vendors, and modelsEvery time someone pastes a customer conversation into an AI, personal data travels: it leaves the company, passes through a vendor, sometimes crosses a border, and sometimes becomes training material. In this lesson, you follow the data's path, check the legal basis and the contract, and fill out the vendor sheet, including for open models.
  7. Lesson 7 · 22 minLightweight governance: the checklist that stays when you leaveFixing today's failures isn't enough: tomorrow a new tool arrives. Governance is the small set of rules and routines the company maintains on its own. For an SMB, it fits in 20 items, drawn from the references the market uses: NIST AI RMF, ISO/IEC 42001, EU AI Act, and LGPD. No committee, no bureaucracy.
  8. Lesson 8 · 25 minReport with evidence, a 30-day plan, and what has changedEverything you gathered is only worth something if leadership understands it and acts. The report has a one-page summary in the owner's language, the findings with proof, what you could not see, and a 30-day plan with an owner and a deadline for each item. At the end, there is a panel of what has changed in the market, updated every quarter.

Who it's for

  1. 1IT, compliance, controllership, or management professionals who want to help companies use AI safely.
  2. 2Consultants who already serve small and mid-sized businesses and get asked, "Is this AI we use safe?".
  3. 3You don't need to code: you read evidence, ask the right questions, and know when to call a specialist.

What you'll be able to do

  1. 1Write the scope agreement and authorization letter before looking at anything.
  2. 2Build the company's AI inventory, including "shadow AI".
  3. 3Classify risks with a matrix based on OWASP (LLM and Agentic) and record the evidence.
  4. 4Review an AI-built app, an agent that takes actions, and a vendor that receives customer data.
  5. 5Deliver a one-page executive report and a 30-day plan.

What this course is NOT

  1. 1AI risk assessment and triage. It is not a pentest (penetration test), nor incident response, nor a legal opinion.
  2. 2When a case calls for that, you escalate to a qualified professional: lesson 1 shows you how.
  3. 3The INEMA certification is a qualification, not a regulated profession, and it does not replace a certified ISO audit.
