Lesson 1 · 18 min

The AI auditor: what you assess, what you don't, and the written authorization

The company already uses AI in places leadership doesn't even know about. Someone needs to look with a method, show the evidence, and say what to do first. That is the AI auditor. Before you open any system, you agree in writing on what you will look at, what you won't, and who authorized it.

Start the lesson

In this lesson you will

  1. 1What an AI auditor delivers (and what they don't)
  2. 2The 6 steps of the work, from authorization to report
  3. 3When to stop and call another professional
  4. 4The scope agreement and authorization, ready to copy

Objective: define the scope of the assessment, say what you don't do, and get the owner's written authorization before looking at any system.

01 · The auditor's work, from start to finish

Six steps. Nothing starts before the first one. Tap each one.

A pentest is really trying to break in; that requires a contract and a dedicated professional. You read, ask, and record.

Scope of this program: assessment and triage of AI risks. It is not a pentest, nor incident response, nor a legal opinion.

02 · What you do and what you don't

Making the limit clear is what gives your work credibility.

1. You do

You map what the company uses in AI, read settings and documents the owner has released, and interview the people who use them.

You rate each risk with evidence and say what to do now, in 7 days, and in 30 days.

At HorizonteYou open the customer spreadsheet with the login Patrícia gave you and note that it is open to "anyone with the link".

2. You do not do

Pentest: you do not try to break in, you do not run attack tools, you do not test anyone's password.

Incident response: if a break-in is happening, you do not investigate alone. Legal opinion: you point out the LGPD (Brazil's data protection law) risk; whether it is illegal is for a lawyer to say.

Ready-made phraseI point out the risk and the evidence. The legal conclusion is up to your lawyer.

3. When to stop

Stop and tell the owner the same day if you find: signs of a break-in in progress, personal data already leaked, a crime, or anything outside the signed scope.

Write down what you saw, don't touch anything, and point to the right professional: security (incident), lawyer (LGPD (Brazil's data protection law)), the vendor's IT.

At HorizonteIn the agent's log you see requests nobody recognizes. You stop, tell Carlos and Rafael (IT), and suggest calling incident response.

4. Never

Never ask for or keep anyone's password: the access owner is the one who types it. Never copy a secret into the report: show only where it is and the first 4 characters.

Never promise "total security". What you could not see becomes "not verified", never "ok".

In the reportAPI key exposed in app.js, line 12 (starts with sk-t…). Not verified: whether the key is still active.

03 · Template to copy: scope agreement and authorization

One page. Fill in the fields between < > and ask for a signature before you start.

Scope agreement and authorization
SCOPE AGREEMENT AND AUTHORIZATION — AI RISK ASSESSMENT

Company: <legal name>            CNPJ: <number>
Authorizing person: <name, title>
Assessor: <your name>            Contact: <email / phone>
Period: from <date> to <date>

1. WHAT THIS WORK IS
Assessment and triage of risks in the use of artificial intelligence.
It is NOT a penetration test (pentest), NOT incident response and
NOT a legal opinion. It does not issue certification.

2. WHAT WILL BE ASSESSED (scope)
[ ] AI tools used by employees (inventory)
[ ] App(s): <name and address>
[ ] Agent(s): <name, channel (WhatsApp, email...)>
[ ] Integrations / connectors (MCP, automations): <which>
[ ] Spreadsheets and databases that feed the AI: <which>
[ ] AI vendors and contracts: <which>

3. WHAT IS OUT OF SCOPE
- <e.g.: third-party systems, customer website, the bank's production environment>

4. HOW THE ASSESSOR WORKS
- Read-only: settings, documents, code and records that have been released.
- Access with a read-only login created by the company; never receives anyone's password.
- Does not change, delete, test attacks, or send anything on behalf of the company.
- Secrets found appear in the report only as location + first 4 characters.
- Personal data seen stays confidential and does not leave the company's environment.

5. WHEN THE ASSESSOR STOPS AND REPORTS (the same day)
- Sign of a break-in in progress or personal data already leaked.
- Indication of a crime.
- Anything outside the scope above.
In these cases they record what they saw, touch nothing and point to
the right professional (security, legal, vendor).

6. DELIVERABLE
One-page executive report + findings with evidence +
unverified items + 30-day plan. Date: <date>.

7. AUTHORIZATION
I authorize the assessment described above, within the scope and period.

_____________________________    Date: ___/___/______
<name of the authorizing person>, <title>

_____________________________    Date: ___/___/______
<name of the assessor>
Simulation: illustrative example
  1. The scope lists each app, agent and integration by name.
  2. "What is out of scope" has at least one item.
  3. The three denials (not a pentest, not incident response, not a legal opinion) are written out.
  4. Whoever signs answers for the company (owner or director), not just the person who called you.
  • Authorization by WhatsApp ("go ahead and take a look"): it doesn't say what or how far. Send the agreement and ask for a signature, even a digital one.
  • Scope "all of IT": impossible to deliver. List it piece by piece.
  • Receiving the owner's password: ask for a read-only login in your name.

Six stepsAuthorization, inventory, triage, review, governance, and report. Nothing starts before authorization.
One-page agreementScope item by item, what stays out, how you work, when you stop, and who signs.

ScopeAssessment and triage of AI risks. This is not a pentest, nor incident response, nor a legal opinion. Sign of an intrusion, a leak, or a legal question: stop and escalate.
