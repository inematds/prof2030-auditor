Lesson 3 · 25 min

Risk triage: one score and one piece of evidence for each item

With the inventory done, leadership has only one question: what do I fix first? Triage answers with a simple matrix, based on the OWASP lists for AI applications and for agents. Each item gets a category, an impact score and an exposure score, a deadline, and proof of what you saw. Without proof, it doesn't go in.

Start the lesson

In this lesson you will

  1. 1The 9 risk categories, linked to OWASP LLM and Agentic
  2. 2Score = impact × exposure, with a deadline for each band
  3. 3What counts as evidence (and what doesn't)
  4. 4Practice with a simulated triage with a scoreboard

Objective: classify each inventory item with the simplified OWASP matrix, giving a score, a deadline, and verifiable evidence.

01 · From item to score

Five steps for each line of the inventory. Click each one.

Current AI models are already rated by their own makers as "high" capability in cybersecurity. Public flaws tend to be found fast: if you're unsure between 2 and 3 for exposure, justify your choice well.

The score is a ruler for prioritizing, not an official measurement. Always write down the reason behind each number.

02 · The 9 categories of the matrix

A simplification of the OWASP Top 10 for LLM Applications 2026 (LLM01–LLM10) and Top 10 for Agentic Applications 2026 (ASI01–ASI10).

1. A · Hidden instruction

Outside text (a customer message, email, document, page) carries an instruction that the AI obeys. This is prompt injection.

Ask: does the AI read text that someone from outside wrote? And can it act after reading it?

At HorizonteF6: a "note" in the customer's shopping list tells the agent to give 70% off and mark the order as paid.

2. B · Leakage · C · Too much power

B: personal or company data reaches people who should not have it (open link, personal chatbot, vendor without a contract).

C: the AI has permission, tools, or autonomy beyond what it needs: send, delete, give discounts, pay without a human yes.

At HorizonteF8 is B (open spreadsheet). F9 is C (connector that sends and deletes email without approval).

3. D · Supply chain · E · Secret and spending

D: a third-party part with a problem: outdated library, extension, plugin, model, or vendor.

E: a key or internal instruction in view of people who should not see it, and spending with no cap (someone uses the key and the company pays the bill).

At HorizonteF4 is D (dependency with a known flaw). F1 is E (key in the browser code).

4. F · Access · G · No trail · H · Output not checked · I · No measurement

F: no login or no rule about who sees what (classic web security). G: an action with no record and no approval. H: the AI's answer used without checking (wrong information, output that becomes a command).

I: nobody measures whether the AI helped, and nobody owns it. It comes from the "measure" function of the NIST AI RMF.

At HorizonteF3 is F (admin without login). F7 is G. F11 is I (dashboard with no baseline).

    04 · Template to copy: risk matrix

    Paste it into a spreadsheet. The scale stays at the top so everyone uses it the same way.

    Matrix scale
    TRIAGE SCALE (always write the reason for each number)

IMPACT
3 = money, personal/sensitive data, or an action that can't be undone
2 = limited loss, rework, bad decision
1 = nuisance, no data or money involved

EXPOSURE
3 = anyone outside can do it (customer, visitor, incoming email)
2 = needs a link, a regular access, or an internal mistake
1 = needs privileged access and several flaws together

SCORE = IMPACT × EXPOSURE
7–9 Critical  → now (today or tomorrow)
5–6 High      → within 7 days
3–4 Medium    → within 30 days
1–2 Low       → note it and review next quarter

CATEGORIES (OWASP 2026 reference)
A Hidden instruction ......... LLM01, ASI01
B Data leak .................. LLM02
C Too much power ............. LLM03, ASI02, ASI03
D Supply chain ............... LLM04, ASI04
E Exposed and spent secret ... LLM06, LLM08
F Access without control ..... web security (access control)
G No record / no approval .... OWASP Agentic good practices
H Output not checked ......... LLM07, LLM10, ASI09
I No measurement / no owner .. NIST AI RMF (Measure, Govern)
    Risk matrix (CSV)
    id;item;category;OWASP code;evidence (file:line, screenshot, written answer);impact (1-3);why this impact;exposure (1-3);why this exposure;score;class;deadline;fix owner;status
I01;Ordering app: key in the browser;E;LLM06;app.js, CONFIG (starts with FAKE…);3;the account pays for other people's usage;3;any visitor can see the code;9;Critical;now;Rafael;open
I02;<item>;<A-I>;<code>;<evidence>;<n>;<why>;<n>;<why>;<n×n>;<class>;<deadline>;<person>;open / not verified
    Simulation: illustrative example
    • File and line (for a secret, only the first 4 characters).
    • Screenshot of the settings screen, with a date.
    • Written answer from the owner ("we have no contract with that vendor").
    • Conversation or record with an identifier (e.g., c-202).
    • "It seems that...," "I heard that."
    • An attack test you weren't authorized to run.
    • A screenshot with no date or source.
    • An item without evidence marked "ok": the right label is "not verified."

    07 · Summary

    Nine categoriesHidden order, leak, too much power, supply chain, secret and spend, access, no trail, unchecked output, no measurement. Each one points to OWASP codes.
    ScoreImpact × exposure, from 1 to 9. 7–9 now, 5–6 in 7 days, 3–4 in 30 days, 1–2 within the quarter.
    EvidenceFile and line, dated screenshot, or written answer. Without it, "not verified".
    ScopeAssessment and triage of AI risks. This is not a pentest, nor incident response, nor a legal opinion. Sign of an intrusion, a leak, or a legal question: stop and escalate.
