Lesson 7 · 22 min

Lightweight governance: the checklist that stays when you leave

Fixing today's failures isn't enough: tomorrow a new tool arrives. Governance is the small set of rules and routines the company maintains on its own. For an SMB, it fits in 20 items, drawn from the references the market uses: NIST AI RMF, ISO/IEC 42001, EU AI Act, and LGPD. No committee, no bureaucracy.

Start the lesson

In this lesson you will

  1. 1What each reference brings to an SMB
  2. 2Governance proportional to risk
  3. 3Baseline and usage policy, the two most common gaps
  4. 4The 20-item checklist, ready to copy

Objective: build the company's 20-item governance checklist, with status, evidence, owner, and deadline, tied to the NIST, ISO 42001, EU AI Act, and LGPD references.

01 · Four references, one checklist

You don't need to roll out all four. Use what each one teaches. Tap each block.

Gartner predicts that AI regulation will reach 75% of the world's economies by 2030. A simple checklist today avoids a rush later.

Scope: you recommend the governance and show the evidence. You do not issue an ISO certificate or say whether the company complies with the law.

02 · Proportional to risk

Too much governance blocks work; too little does not protect. The size follows the matrix from lesson 3.

1. Low-risk use

Written usage policy, human review of anything that goes outside, and an incident log.

E.g., a text proofreader on an internal announcement.

Policy in 3 linesWhat is allowed. What is not (customer data on a personal account, for example). Who to ask for a new tool.

2. Medium risk

Everything from low, plus a named owner, monitoring with an indicator and baseline, and a review of the log.

E.g., a dashboard that decides whether the agent expands to collections.

At HorizonteF11: the dashboard started on the same day as the agent. Without the "before," no one can prove it helped.

3. High risk

Everything from medium, plus mandatory human approval on sensitive actions, an audit trail, test cases before any change, and a legal review.

E.g., an agent that creates orders and gives discounts; an integration that sends email.

At HorizonteHora agent and email assistant: approval above R$ 5 mil, never delete, log reviewed every week.

4. The shadow

Shadow AI is not solved by banning it: take stock, assess each tool, offer an approved alternative, and publish the policy.

Repeat the internal survey every quarter.

At HorizonteF12: the extension that reads emails and the app that records visits without notice go into the inventory and the policy, with an alternative.

Simulation: illustrative example
  1. There are 20 lines, each with a status.
  2. A "yes" always has evidence (document, screenshot, calendar entry).
  3. Every "no" and "partial" has an owner and a due date.
  4. The reference is on the line, for anyone who wants to go deeper.
  • Copying a whole standard: a small business doesn't maintain 200 controls. It maintains 20.
  • A "yes" without evidence: becomes "not verified".
  • Promising compliance: the checklist shows practices; legal compliance is a matter for legal, and ISO certification is a matter for an accredited body.

06 · Summary

Four referencesNIST AI RMF gives the 4 functions, ISO/IEC 42001 the management system, the AI Act the risk-based yardstick (Annex III high risk on 02/12/2027), and LGPD (Brazil's data protection law) the rule that already applies.
ProportionalLow risk: policy, review and log. Medium: more owners and measures. High: more approval, audit trail, tests and legal.
Twenty itemsGovern 6, map 4, measure 5, manage 5. Status, evidence, owner and due date. Quarterly review.
ScopeAssessment and triage of AI risks. This is not a pentest, nor incident response, nor a legal opinion. Sign of an intrusion, a leak, or a legal question: stop and escalate.
