Lesson 2 · 20 min

AI inventory: everything the company uses, with an owner

No one protects what they don't know exists. The inventory is the list of everything that uses AI in the company: apps, agents, integrations, spreadsheets that feed the AI, and the tools employees use on their own. Every row has an owner. Without an inventory, the triage in lesson 3 becomes a guess.

Start the lesson

In this lesson you will

  1. 1The 6 places where AI shows up in a small business
  2. 2How to find "shadow AI" without breaking into anything
  3. 3The columns triage will need later
  4. 4The inventory spreadsheet, ready to copy

Objective: list apps, agents, integrations, MCP connectors, and the company's shadow AI, with one owner per item, in a single spreadsheet.

01 · Where AI shows up in an SMB

Six places. Each one becomes rows in the inventory. Tap to see what to look for.

Scope: you collect information and ask questions. You do not install spy software, and you do not read anyone's email.

Old integrations count too: a connector nobody updates keeps its access until someone turns it off.

02 · How to collect it, in four moves

Nothing here requires breaking in. It is all asking questions and looking at dashboards with the access owner next to you.

1. Ask

Send a 5-question questionnaire to everyone, with no witch hunt: "what AI do you use at work, for what, with what data, on which account?".

Say that the goal is to protect, not to punish. Without that, shadow AI stays hidden.

At HorizonteIn the survey, someone in Sales says they paste the customer spreadsheet into a free chatbot, on a personal account, to write proposals. IT did not know.

2. Check accounts

In the company's Google or Microsoft account, the administrator shows the "connected apps": every AI app that asked for access to email or Drive appears there.

Also ask for the corporate card statement for the last 3 months: an AI subscription shows up as a charge.

RequestRafael, can you open the connected apps screen of the company account while I take notes?

3. Check connectors

For each agent and integration, open the settings screen with the person who administers it and write down what it can do: read, create, send, delete, pay.

This also applies to browser extensions: what they ask for has to match what they promise.

At HorizonteThe MCP integration with email has permission to send and delete messages. This goes in the "actions" column.

4. Confirm the owner

Every item needs an owner: the person who answers if something goes wrong and who can turn it off.

An item without an owner is a finding in itself. Write down "no owner" and take it to the report.

RuleThe owner is a person with a name, not "sales" or "IT."

Questionnaire for employees (5 questions)
We are mapping the company's AI tools so we can use them safely.
No one will be punished for answering. It takes 3 minutes.

1. What AI tools do you use at work? (chat, extension,
   meeting transcription, image generator, other)
2. What for? Give an example of a task.
3. Do you put customer, employee, or financial data into them?
   [ ] no  [ ] sometimes  [ ] always  — what type?
4. Is the account the company's or personal? Paid or free?
5. Does any of them have access to your email, calendar, Drive, or WhatsApp?
Simulation: illustrative example
  1. Every item has an owner with a person's name (or the finding "no owner").
  2. The "actions" column uses verbs: read, create, send, delete, pay.
  3. Each row says where the information came from.
  4. There is at least one item with "IT knows? no".
  • Only what IT knows: the inventory comes out neat and incomplete. Shadow AI shows up in the questionnaire and in the invoice.
  • Writing down what the AI says, not what it does: the risk is in the actions (send, delete, give a discount).
  • A "verify" that is never verified: in the report it becomes "not verified," with the reason.

06 · Summary

Six placesApps built by AI, agents, integrations and MCP, shadow AI, databases and spreadsheets, vendors.
Four movesAsk, check accounts, check connectors, confirm the owner.
Why shadow AI mattersIn May 2026, researchers studying shadow AI found about 380,000 public apps built on vibe coding platforms, around 5,000 of them with sensitive corporate data (recorded on OECD.AI, based on Axios).
ScopeAssessment and triage of AI risks. This is not a pentest, nor incident response, nor a legal opinion. Sign of an intrusion, a leak, or a legal question: stop and escalate.
